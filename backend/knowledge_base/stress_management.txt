# Mental Wellness Psychoeducation: Stress Management for Higher Education

Title: Evidence-Based Stress Management for College Students
Category: Stress Management
Audience: Undergraduate and Graduate Students

## Understanding College Stress
Stress is a natural physiological reaction to demanding situations, such as exams, project deadlines, financial pressure, or transitioning to university life. When the body encounters a stressor, the sympathetic nervous system triggers the "fight-or-flight" response, releasing cortisol and adrenaline. While acute stress can temporarily boost alertness, chronic stress impairs concentration, disrupts sleep, and weakens immune function.

## Evidence-Based Coping Strategies
1. Time Blocking and Cognitive Load Reduction:
Break academic assignments into manageable chunks using the Pomodoro Technique (25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break). Prioritize tasks using an Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent vs. Important).

2. Physiological Sighs:
Developed by neurobiology researchers, two quick inhales through the nose followed by a long, slow exhale through the mouth rapidly reduces autonomic nervous system arousal within 30 seconds.

3. Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR):
Systematically tense each muscle group (feet, calves, thighs, shoulders, face) for 5 seconds, then deliberately release the tension for 15 seconds. This biofeedback breaks the physical loop of stress-induced tension.

4. Boundaries and Saying No:
Overcommitting to extracurricular clubs, social events, and excessive course credits leads to academic exhaustion. Establish clear boundaries around study hours and rest times.

## When to Seek Support
If stress interferes with your ability to attend lectures, complete assignments, or enjoy daily activities for more than two consecutive weeks, schedule an appointment with your campus counseling center.
