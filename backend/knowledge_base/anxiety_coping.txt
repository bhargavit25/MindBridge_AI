# Mental Wellness Psychoeducation: Anxiety Coping Strategies & Grounding

Title: Managing Anxiety and Panic in Academic Settings
Category: Anxiety Coping Strategies
Audience: Higher Education Students

## What is Anxiety?
Anxiety is characterized by persistent, excessive worries about everyday circumstances, often accompanied by physical symptoms like rapid heart rate, shallow breathing, sweating, or restlessness. In students, performance anxiety, test anxiety, and social anxiety are frequent experiences.

## Immediate Grounding Techniques for Acute Anxiety:
1. The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Technique:
Engage your immediate senses to bring your nervous system out of an anxious spiral and back into the present moment:
- 5 things you can SEE around you (e.g., pen, laptop screen, cloud, chair, door).
- 4 things you can physically FEEL (e.g., feet on the floor, texture of your jeans, breeze).
- 3 things you can HEAR (e.g., hum of the air conditioner, distant traffic, quiet typing).
- 2 things you can SMELL (e.g., coffee, fresh air, notebook paper).
- 1 thing you can TASTE (e.g., mint, water, or acknowledge your current tongue position).

2. Box Breathing (4x4):
Used widely in high-stress environments:
- Inhale slowly for 4 seconds.
- Hold breath gently for 4 seconds.
- Exhale smoothly for 4 seconds.
- Hold empty lungs gently for 4 seconds.
- Repeat 4 cycles.

3. Cognitive Reframing (CBT Principle):
Notice catastrophic "what if" thoughts (e.g., "What if I fail this exam and ruin my future?"). Ask:
- What is the realistic worst-case, best-case, and most likely outcome?
- Have I survived difficult exams or presentations before?
- What is one constructive action I can take right now?
