# Mental Wellness Psychoeducation: Sleep Hygiene & Cognitive Recovery

Title: Optimizing Sleep for Cognitive Performance and Emotional Health
Category: Sleep Hygiene
Audience: College Students

## The Relationship Between Sleep and Mental Health
Sleep is not passive downtime; it is an active neurobiological state during which the brain clears metabolic waste products and consolidates memory learned during study sessions. Chronic sleep deprivation increases emotional reactivity by up to 60%, exaggerates depressive symptoms, and impairs working memory.

## Essential Sleep Hygiene Rules for University Life:
1. Consistent Wake-Up Time:
Wake up at the same hour (+/- 30 minutes) 7 days a week, regardless of when you went to bed. This stabilizes your circadian circadian pacemaker.

2. Morning Light Exposure:
Expose your eyes to outdoor sunlight within 30-60 minutes of waking for 10-15 minutes. This sets the internal clock for nocturnal melatonin release approximately 14-16 hours later.

3. The Caffeine Curfew:
Adenosine builds up throughout the day creating "sleep pressure". Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors without eliminating adenosine. Avoid caffeine 8-10 hours prior to your target bedtime.

4. Bed As Sleep Sanctuary:
Avoid studying, eating, or arguing in bed. Condition your brain to associate the mattress exclusively with sleep and relaxation.

5. Screen Wind-Down:
Blue spectrum light from smartphones and monitors suppresses melatonin secretion. Discontinue active device use 45 minutes before sleep, or use night-shift warm color modes.
